# Incident 02: Customer match rate fell after a CRM upgrade

> Generated by `casebook run 2`. Every number below is computed from the data.

## Symptom

The share of CRM contacts linked to a billing customer fell from 97.8% to 57.0%. Revenue per customer and churn reports started to look wrong.

## Investigation

| # | Hypothesis | Check | Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Billing keys changed | Billing keys that break the `AA-999999` shape | 0 of 40,000 | **rejected** |
| 2 | The unmatched contacts are new customers | Unmatched CRM keys with a billing twin once normalized | 16,672 of 17,572 (94.9%) already exist in billing | **rejected** |
| 3 | The CRM changed its key format | Key shapes in CRM, before vs after the upgrade date | before: `AA-999999` · after: `AA_99999`, `AA_9999`, `AA_999`; match rate before upgrade 97.8% | **confirmed** |

## Root cause

After the upgrade the CRM exports keys as `pe_123` instead of `PE-000123`: lower case, underscore, no zero padding. Only contacts touched after the upgrade changed, so the drop grew day by day instead of appearing all at once.

## Fix

A `normalize_key()` macro (upper-case country, dash, 6-digit zero-padded number) applied in staging to every source, so both sides are compared in one format.

## Guard

A contract check fails the build if any staged key breaks the shape `^[A-Z]{2}-[0-9]{6}$`, and the match rate is tracked daily so a slow slide is visible.

## Before and after

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| CRM contacts matched to billing | 57.0% | 97.8% |
| Unmatched contacts | 17,572 | 900 |
| Previously unmatched contacts recovered | – | 94.9% |
| Staged keys breaking the format contract | not checked | 0 |
